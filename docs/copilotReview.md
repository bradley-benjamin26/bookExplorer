# Copilot User Report

## Method

These are **persona-based walkthroughs**, not observed live usability tests. The app was run locally on the web (backend server and Expo web build). Eleven screens were captured at phone size with real data:

- Home and barcode scanner
- Book page for *Dune*, before and after saving
- "Science Fiction" subject page
- Frank Herbert author page
- Graph view
- Search results for "becoming michelle obama"
- My Library
- Editing a saved book
- Find Connection

Each participant was a separate agent. It read a written walkthrough of those screens and viewed every screenshot. No physical barcode was scanned, because the web scanner stops at a camera-permission prompt. The walkthrough pointed out several data problems in advance, so some of the shared complaints partly reflect that briefing rather than independent discovery.

The brief listed six profiles, two of them labeled "Participant 3". Both are included and are marked 3a and 3b. The app's on-screen title is still **Book Explorer**, while the name question asks about **Bookweft**.

## Participant feedback

### Participant 1 — 13, frequent library visitor

**What I tried:** Scanning a library book, then exploring *Dune*'s subjects, Frank Herbert's page, and the graph. Also search, saving and editing a book, library-link setup, and export options.

1. **Overall design:** I like the dark colors and the big book covers. It feels cozy and bookish. The graph looks fun, but some screens seem crowded and the book data can be pretty messy.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Mostly easy, because the main buttons like "Scan a book" and "My Library" are clear. The graph and all the subject tags might confuse me. The camera-permission screen means scanning isn't instant.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes, to find books with similar subjects or authors. But the Science Fiction page mostly shows older books, and I'd want newer ones too.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** Saving books I want to read and writing notes about them. Seeing what else an author wrote, and finding books my library might have.
5. **What don't you like?** Study guides and other knock-offs appear before the real *Becoming* in search. The odd tags, and *House Corrino* credited to the wrong authors, make me less sure I can trust everything.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Cozy, curious, colorful, crowded, bookish, tangled.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** It sounds pretty, like books woven together, which fits finding connections. I'm not sure what "weft" means, though, so "Book Explorer" is easier to understand right away.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Trails**, because it sounds like following a path from one book to another.

### Participant 2 — 18, comics and sci-fi fan

**What I tried:** Scanning *Dune*, then exploring Science Fiction and its narrower topics, Frank Herbert's page, the graph, and Find Connection. Also the saved-book and editing screens, search, library, and export.

1. **Overall design:** The dark look, book covers, and big headings make it feel like a place to browse, not a plain catalog. The graph is a cool idea, but the tiny, crowded labels could get messy fast.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Mostly easy: scan or search, tap a subject, save a book. I'd need a minute to figure out the graph and filters. The header and footer overlapping the page look annoying on a phone.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yeah, especially through narrower topics like Cyberpunk or Science Fiction Comics. But the Science Fiction list is heavy on old classics, so I'd want better modern picks.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** Keeping track of books I want to read, adding my own tags and notes, and finding connections between books or authors. Exporting to Goodreads or StoryGraph would be handy.
5. **What don't you like?** Some subject labels look like raw database entries. The mixed-up *House Corrino* author makes me question the recommendations. Search results full of study guides and knock-offs are frustrating, and the library settings sound technical.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Curious, dark, nerdy, tangled, useful.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** It sounds creative, like books woven together, which fits the graph idea. But it doesn't immediately say it's for discovering books, and the app still calls itself "Book Explorer" on screen.
8. **What would you name it?** **Shelf Space**, because it sounds relaxed and leaves room to explore without feeling library-only.

### Participant 3a — 22, recent college graduate

**What I tried:** Scanning or searching for a book, saving *Dune*, and exploring its subjects, author page, and graph. Also My Library, Find Connection, and the Goodreads/StoryGraph export options.

1. **Overall design:** I like it. The dark background, bookish headings, and colorful covers feel cozy and focused. Some screens feel cramped, though, and the top buttons overlap page titles, which makes the polish uneven.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Fairly easy for the basics: scan, search, save, and tap through to an author or subject. Once I'm exploring, the raw subject labels and crowded graph make it harder to tell what's useful.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes, especially while browsing in a bookstore. I'd want more current, relevant results first. The Science Fiction list leans toward older classics, and search shows knock-offs before the real *Becoming*.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** Keeping track of books I'm curious about with my own tags and notes. Finding connections between books sounds fun. Library links and export would help me move saved books to another service.
5. **What don't you like?** Some book data looks messy, with duplicate or overly technical subject labels. I'd worry about inaccurate recommendations, like the wrong author on *House Corrino*. I also wish I could make separate lists instead of one saved-books collection.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Cozy, curious, connected, cluttered, promising, bookish.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** A pretty fitting name, because the app weaves books together through authors, subjects, and shared links. It's distinctive but doesn't immediately say the app helps me discover books.
8. **What would you name it?** **Booktrail**, because it suggests following a trail from one book to another, which is the part I find most appealing.

### Participant 3b — 35, working new parent

**What I tried:** Searching for *Becoming* by Michelle Obama, then following *Dune* to its subject, author, and graph. Also saving, library recommendations, editing notes, and export options. The scanner asked for camera permission.

1. **Overall design:** I like the dark colors and big serif headings, which feel bookish and polished. Some screens look crowded, and the header and footer can get in the way on a small phone.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Searching and saving are easy to find. It's less comfortable one-handed when I have to scroll or tap through crowded content, and the scanner needs camera access.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes, especially by following an author or subject I already like. The *Becoming* results were frustrating: the real book was there but came after a study guide.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** Keeping track of books I want to read and jotting quick notes when I have a moment. Exporting to Goodreads or StoryGraph could be handy.
5. **What don't you like?** Subject pages mix useful topics with odd cataloging terms. The Science Fiction list was mostly older books, which isn't great when I want something current. I'd double-check recommendations, since *House Corrino* was credited to the wrong author.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Bookish, dark, curious, connected, cluttered, practical.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** It makes me think of weaving books and ideas together, which fits the links between books, authors, and subjects. It's not immediately obvious what the app does, though.
8. **What would you name it?** **Next Chapter**, because it feels welcoming and suggests finding what to read next.

### Participant 4 — 68, retired and joining book clubs

**What I tried:** Scanning or searching for a book, opening *Dune*, exploring its subject and graph, and saving it. Also the library, notes, library-link, and export options. The scanner asked for camera permission, and the graph looked a little crowded.

1. **Overall design:** I like the dark look and big headings, and the bright buttons are easy to spot. Some smaller, dimmer text and busy screens are harder for me.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Mostly easy, though I'd need a moment to understand the graph and the technical library-link instructions. Labels like "Scan a book" and "My Library" are clear.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. I'd like finding books through authors and subjects, though the Science Fiction list was heavy on older titles.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** Saving book club picks and adding notes for our discussions. Exporting could be useful, but I'd want named collections for different clubs.
5. **What don't you like?** Search showed several knock-offs before *Becoming*, and some subject labels look like messy catalog notes. The recommendation also credited *House Corrino* to the wrong authors.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Curious, dark, bookish, crowded, useful.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** It could fit, because it suggests weaving connections between books, but I wouldn't know what it meant at first.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Connections**, because it tells me what the app does.

### Participant 5 — 29, heavy Goodreads user

**What I tried:** Scanning and searching, then exploring *Dune*'s subjects, Frank Herbert's page, the graph, and Find Connection. Also recommendations, library editing, and CSV import/export. No file was imported and no barcode was scanned, because the scanner asks for camera permission.

1. **Overall design:** I like the dark, bookish look, and the big covers and graph give it personality. It feels more like a discovery tool than a polished replacement for Goodreads or StoryGraph. Pages get crowded and the library data has rough edges.
2. **Easy or difficult?** The main actions are easy to spot and saving is straightforward. I'd call it moderately easy rather than effortless. The sticky header and footer crowd small screens, and long, messy subject labels make exploring less smooth.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** For following a specific book or author into related subjects and connections, browsing sideways rather than getting a "similar books" list, yes. I wouldn't rely on its recommendations yet. The Science Fiction list skews old, *Becoming* search puts a study guide first, and *House Corrino* has the wrong author.
4. **Other ways you'd use it:** A personal catalog I can export or back up, with my own subjects, genres, and notes. Find Connection could be fun for tracing links between two books. The library search link could help me find copies to borrow.
5. **What don't you like?** The metadata needs cleaning: "Science Fiction" and "Science-Fiction" both appear, alongside cataloging labels that aren't useful for browsing. I'd miss named lists. The book page I saw had no plot description and no obvious "read / want to read" status.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Bookish, exploratory, cluttered, graphy, promising, uneven.
7. **Is "Bookweft" appropriate?** A pretty good fit, since it suggests weaving connections between books, authors, and subjects, which is the most distinctive part. It doesn't immediately say it's a discovery app, and the screen still says "Book Explorer", so the name and product identity should line up.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Threads**, because it keeps the sense of connections but sounds more approachable.

## What came up across participants

- **Design:** All six liked the dark, bookish look and the covers. All six called screens crowded or cluttered. Four mentioned the sticky header and footer overlapping content on a phone.
- **Ease of use:** Everyone found the main actions (scan, search, save) easy. The graph and the library-link setup were the hardest parts.
- **Finding books:** All six said yes, but most wanted more modern books in the subject lists and more accurate results.
- **Complaints:**
  - Messy or duplicate subject labels came up in every interview.
  - All six pointed out the wrong author on *House Corrino* (it is by Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson).
  - All six complained about knock-offs ranking above the real *Becoming* in search.
  - Three asked for multiple named lists, which is part of the app's stated goal but doesn't exist yet.
- **"Bookweft":** Everyone liked the "woven together" meaning. Five of six said it doesn't tell you what the app does, and the 13-year-old didn't know what "weft" means. Two noticed the screen still says "Book Explorer".
- **Suggested names:** Book Trails / Booktrail (two votes), Next Chapter, Shelf Space, Book Connections, Book Threads. Most of them, like "Bookweft", are about following connections between books.
