# Luna6 User Report

## Participant feedback

These are **persona-based, source-level walkthroughs**, not observed live usability tests: the app could not be launched in this environment. The six profiles include both entries labeled “Participant 3”; they are presented separately. The current interface calls the app **Book Explorer**, while the name question refers to **Bookweft**.

### Participant 1 — 13, frequent library visitor

1. **Overall design:** Calm and bookish. The “Scan a book” button stands out, though the book page may have a lot to scroll through.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Mostly easy. Scanning, searching, and tapping subject tags seem straightforward. Setting up a library catalog link looks confusing because it involves pasting search URLs.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. I’d scan a library book and look for more by that author or with similar subjects.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Save books for later, browse recommendations, check library availability, and import or export book data.
5. **What don’t you like?** Recommendations would need to prove useful. I don’t see separate lists for different purposes, and setting up library links might require help.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Books, library, exploring, discovery, quiet.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** It suggests books are woven together, which fits the connections, but “weft” may be unfamiliar and doesn’t explain the app’s purpose.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Finder**, because it’s simple and clear.

### Participant 2 — 18, comics and sci-fi reader

1. **Overall design:** Clean and focused. The scan button is easy to spot, and book covers, titles, and authors are up front.
2. **Easy or difficult?** The basic path seems easy: scan or search, open a book, and explore subjects. Filters and graph navigation may take some exploring.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. I’d follow subjects, series, and authors from a comic or sci-fi book. Since I don’t visit libraries much, title search matters too.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Save books for later, look up authors, explore series, and export a list to Goodreads or StoryGraph.
5. **What don’t you like?** The interface says “Book Explorer,” so “Bookweft” feels disconnected. I’d also want to see whether recommendations work well for comics and graphic novels.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Books, discovery, exploring, lists, library, graph.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** It suggests connections between books, but doesn’t explain the app by itself and might be hard to spell from hearing it.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Finder**.

### Participant 3 — 22, recent graduate with limited reading time

1. **Overall design:** Calm and book-focused. Scanning and searching are easy to find, though detail screens have a lot of information.
2. **Easy or difficult?** The main flow seems easy: search, open a book, and save it. I’d need to explore to understand the graph and connection features.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. Following subjects, authors, series, or other books by the same author feels useful when I have a little time.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Save books, add notes, check recommendations based on saved titles, and use library links.
5. **What don’t you like?** I’m unsure whether the product is called Book Explorer or Bookweft. I’d want Goodreads or StoryGraph export to be easy to find.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Calm, curious, organized, a little technical.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** It’s distinctive and suggests books woven together, but doesn’t immediately say the app helps find related reads.
8. **What would you name it?** **Next Chapter**.

### Participant 3 — 35, working new parent

1. **Overall design:** Calm and bookish. The home screen feels simple, with more features available once I explore.
2. **Easy or difficult?** The main flow—scan or search, open a book, save it—seems easy. I might need time to find CSV export in My Library.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. I could scan a book and browse related subjects or other books by the same author when I have a few minutes.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Save books, add notes or tags, browse recommendations, or find titles for a work or parenting book club.
5. **What don’t you like?** My Library looks like one collection rather than separate lists. Export is tucked into Backup; I’d like clearer Goodreads and StoryGraph export options.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Calm, useful, curious, bookish, organized.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** It fits the idea of connecting books through authors and subjects, but it’s abstract and doesn’t immediately explain the app.
8. **What would you name it?** **Next Chapter**.

### Participant 4 — 68, retired and joining book clubs

1. **Overall design:** The idea feels thoughtful and welcoming. I like being able to follow subjects, authors, series, and related books, though My Library has many sections.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Scanning or typing a title, author, or ISBN seems simple. I’d need a moment to learn that subject tags are clickable and to understand the graph features.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes, especially before book club or after finding a book at the library. Catalog links could help me locate a copy.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Follow authors, browse subjects and series, keep book-club picks, add notes or tags, and back up the library.
5. **What don’t you like?** My Library is a long page with saved books, recommendations, appearance, library setup, and backup. The catalog URL instructions and graph terms feel technical. I’d also like separate book-club lists.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Inviting, curious, useful, bookish, a little complicated, exploratory.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** I like the meaning of weaving books together once it’s explained, but “weft” may be unfamiliar to new users.
8. **What would you name it?** **Next Chapter**. “Book Explorer” is also clearer than “Bookweft”; a subtitle like “Scan a book. Find your next read.” could explain either name.

### Participant 5 — 29, voracious reader and Goodreads power user

1. **Overall design:** Calm, readable, and focused on connections. It feels more like a book reference tool than a social reading app.
2. **Easy or difficult?** Scanning or searching, inspecting, and saving seem straightforward. Recommendations and export are farther down the long My Library screen; graph features may need explanation.
3. **Would you use it to find books?** Yes. Subject tags, author links, series, and recommendations fit how I discover books. I’d want to judge the metadata quality in practice.
4. **Other ways you’d use it:** Map an author’s work, check series order, back up saved books, and import a Goodreads or StoryGraph list.
5. **What don’t you like?** I’d want to know exactly what carries over in CSV. The export appears to put subjects and genres in Goodreads’ Bookshelves field, but not reading status or ratings; import relies on ISBN matches. I’d also move export higher on the page.
6. **Words that come to mind:** Curious, connected, reference-like, quiet, metadata-heavy.
7. **Is “Bookweft” appropriate?** It suggests books woven together, which fits. But the interface says “Book Explorer,” and “Bookweft” alone doesn’t immediately signal books.
8. **What would you name it?** **Book Explorer** for clarity. I’d consider **Bookweft** if it were used consistently and its connection to books were made clear.

## Patterns across participants

Participants generally liked the clear scan/search starting point and the ability to explore from a book through subjects, authors, and series. Several saw potential in saving books, notes, recommendations, and library lookup.

Common friction points were the long My Library screen, the lack of separate named lists, and technical-feeling library catalog setup. **Bookweft** appealed as a metaphor for books connected together, but several participants found it abstract; the existing **Book Explorer** name was clearer to some.

One feature detail surfaced during the walkthroughs: the app’s CSV export uses Goodreads-style columns; the interface describes importing Goodreads or StoryGraph CSVs. Participants wanted export options to be easier to find and clearer about what data carries over.
