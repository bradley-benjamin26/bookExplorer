# Opus User Review

## Method

These are **simulated, persona-based reviews**, not observed tests with real people. Six AI agents each played one participant profile. The brief listed two profiles as "Participant 3", so both appear here as P3a and P3b.

Each participant went through the same recorded session of the real app: the Expo web build at iPhone size (390×844), backed by the dev server with live data. The session covered:

- Home screen, and search for "project hail mary"
- Book pages for Dune, Saga Vol. 1 and Atomic Habits, saving Dune and Atomic Habits
- Author page (Frank Herbert) and subject page (Science Fiction)
- Graph view and Find Connection
- Scan screen (camera permission prompt only)
- My Library, including the Edit panel for a saved book

The camera scan flow and batch scan were **not** exercised. The interface currently calls the app **Book Explorer**; question 7 asks about **Bookweft**.

| ID | Profile |
|---|---|
| P1 | 13, loves reading, goes to the library often with her parents |
| P2 | 18, nerdy, reads comics and sci-fi, rarely goes to the library, not planning to attend college |
| P3a | 22, recent college graduate, busy with work, making time to read |
| P3b | 35, working new parent, little reading time, likes biographies, business and self-help |
| P4 | 68, retired, some trouble with new technology but a quick study, joining book clubs |
| P5 | 29, heavy Goodreads user, voracious reader, has used many reading apps |

## Responses by question

### 1) What is your overall opinion on the design of the app?

- **P1:** Pretty and grown-up, like a cozy night-time reading app, and the big covers look nice. Book pages turn into "a big pile of brown bubbles," and some, like "Nyt:Mass-Market-Monthly=2021-11-07", look like computer code.
- **P2:** Clean dark mode, and the Dune cover "looked sick." Scrolling shows "raw database junk" tags. The graph looked like a skill tree, but its labels are tiny and the header is cut off to "Explore Gra."
- **P3a:** Cozy, with a "bookstore-at-night vibe." It gets messy fast: a wall of tags with "OL"/"WD" labels she didn't understand, and the graph title cut off behind the buttons.
- **P3b:** Clean and grown-up, more like a nice bookstore than a busy app. Book pages become "a wall of little orange chips" that read like code.
- **P4:** Handsome, calm and bookish, and the big "Scan a book" button made the first step obvious. Bubbles with computer-looking labels and "OL", "WD" and "LC" tags meant nothing to her.
- **P5:** Cozier than "Goodreads' tired beige." "Pretty frame, messy data inside." Atomic Habits has no author link.

### 2) Do you find it easy or difficult to use?

- **P1:** The basics are easy, and Save becoming "★ Saved" is clear. She didn't understand the OL/WD/LC letters or Find Connection. The theme setting and export are hidden at the bottom of My Library.
- **P2:** The basics are easy. Find Connection gave "no clue what it does," and five versions of the same subject made it unclear which to tap.
- **P3a:** The basics are easy. Find Connection opened an empty box with no explanation, and the graph mostly repeated the same subjects.
- **P3b:** Easy enough to save one-handed while holding the baby. The graph was "a spider web of dots," Find Connection was unexplained, and she had to scroll past settings to reach Export.
- **P4:** The basics were easy, and "Saved" reassured her. Find Connection and the graph's "swipe through them with a screen reader" text confused her. She'd want her granddaughter beside her the first time.
- **P5:** Navigation is easy and buttons are where she expected. What's hard is knowing what each screen is for: Find Connection, and graph legend entries (Genre, "Not yet linked") that never appeared.

### 3) Would you use this app to find new books?

- **P1:** Maybe, mainly for "more by this author" and series. Science Fiction suggested Alice in Wonderland, Dracula and French Jules Verne, so she'd still ask her librarian or check BookTok.
- **P2:** Kinda. The author page, with the Dune sequels and "Influenced," and the Cyberpunk / Space Opera / Science Fiction Comics chips were good rabbit holes. The suggested books (Alice in Wonderland, The Wizard of Oz) weren't.
- **P3a:** Maybe, if suggestions improve. Science Fiction gave "basically my high school reading list." The Frank Herbert page was the most useful part.
- **P3b:** Maybe, but it did poorly for her genres. Atomic Habits had three thin subjects, no author and no "if you liked this" list.
- **P4:** Yes, a bit. The author page's short biography and the whole Dune series in order are what she'd want for a book club. Science Fiction showed only old classics.
- **P5:** Not as her main discovery tool. Science Fiction showed only public-domain classics, and StoryGraph gives mood and pace picks. She'd use it for "more by this author" and "what's in this series."

### 4) Are there other ways you would use this app?

- **P1:** Scan library books before checking them out to see the rating and series. Use My Library and Notes as a want-to-read list. Batch scan her stack at checkout.
- **P2:** Scan in a store or comic shop to check the rating and series before buying. Batch scan to log his comic shelf. Export is cool, but he'd never touch the library features.
- **P3a:** A want-to-read list. Scan, save, then export to Goodreads or StoryGraph, which she uses. Notes and the Bookshop.org and library links are useful.
- **P3b:** A quick "save for later" list exported via CSV. Batch scan the shelf during a nap. Bookshop.org, WorldCat or her library to get the book.
- **P4:** Scan and save in bookstores so she doesn't forget, and keep book club notes. She'd export to Goodreads or StoryGraph once someone shows her what a CSV is. Adding her public library appeals, though the explanation lost her.
- **P5:** Scan at used bookstores and library sales. WorldCat and her own library's search link as a Libby-like shortcut. Batch scan to catalogue shelves. Import her Goodreads/StoryGraph CSV and browse it as a graph.

### 5) What don't you like about this app?

- **P1:** Search results full of "Summary of…" and study guides, then Shakespeare. No covers in My Library or "More by Frank Herbert." "Recently looked at" shows numbers instead of titles.
- **P2:** Saga is credited only to Brian K. Vaughan, without Fiona Staples, and tagged with things like "Parents of Racially Mixed Children" instead of space opera. Blank cover boxes. ISBNs in "Recently looked at." The library-URL paragraph.
- **P3a:** Search knockoffs and Shakespeare. Blank covers in "More by Frank Herbert." No suggestions for Atomic Habits. ISBNs in "Recently looked at."
- **P3b:** Too much raw data and not enough recommending: duplicate chips like "Dune (Imaginary Place)" and "Dune (Imaginary Place), Fiction." No author on Atomic Habits. ISBNs in "Recently looked at." My Library mixes saved books with settings and import/export.
- **P4:** The Dune page never says what the book is about, and Reviews are collapsed at the bottom. ISBNs in "Recently looked at." In My Library, the "More by Frank Herbert" list pushes the export buttons far down.
- **P5:** Raw subject tags. Export CSV puts every subject into Goodreads' Bookshelves column. No read / to-read / currently-reading status or star rating. ISBNs in "Recently looked at." Saga has no "Vol. 1" or series info. Blank cover boxes.

### 6) What words come to mind when you think about this app?

- **P1:** cozy, dark, nerdy, library-ish, cluttered, kind of complicated, cool graph
- **P2:** nerdy, dark mode, Wikipedia-ish, rabbit hole, cluttered tags, "library-core"
- **P3a:** moody, cozy, nerdy, librarian-y, cluttered, promising
- **P3b:** tidy, nerdy, library-catalog, a little overwhelming, sci-fi-leaning, "a lot of homework"
- **P4:** handsome, clever, cluttered, technical, promising, a librarian's tool
- **P5:** moody, librarian-y, nerdy, cluttered, promising, metadata, rabbit hole

The most repeated words were *library/librarian-ish* (6), *nerdy* (5), *cluttered* (5), *cozy/moody* (4) and *promising* (3).

### 7) Do you find "Bookweft" an appropriate name? Why or why not?

Five said no. P5 said "kind of."

- **P1:** No. She had to think about what "weft" means, and it sounds like "bereft" or a typo. No one her age would know it or be able to spell it.
- **P2:** No. He had to look up "weft." He gets it once it's explained, but it sounds like a typo and isn't something he'd tell friends to download.
- **P3a:** Not really. "Weft" needed thinking about and she'd misspell it in the App Store. It's clever once explained, but it doesn't say "find your next read."
- **P3b:** No. She has no time to decode a name. She sort of sees the "books woven together" idea, but she'd misspell it.
- **P4:** Not really. Her book club friends wouldn't catch the weaving idea or be able to find it in the app store. "Book Explorer" says plainly what it does.
- **P5:** Kind of. The weaving idea fits the graph and is clever, but most readers won't know "weft," and it sounds like "bereft" or a craft app. It's still more distinctive than "Book Explorer," which sounds like a placeholder.

### 8) What would you name the app?

| Participant | Suggestions | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Book Trail, Next Chapter | Hopping from Dune to Frank Herbert to Dune Messiah feels like following a trail |
| P2 | Book Web, Shelfscan, Lore | "Digging up the lore on a book" |
| P3a | Next Read, Shelf Hop | You hop from one book you like to the next |
| P3b | Next Read, ShelfScan | Plain and says what it does; "Book Explorer" also works |
| P4 | Next Read | It's already the tagline and exactly why she'd open the app |
| P5 | Booktrails, Shelfthread | Says "connections" without a dictionary |

The favourites were **Next Read** (3, taken from the existing "find your next read" tagline), **Book Trail(s)** (2) and **ShelfScan** (2).

## What participants liked most

- The Frank Herbert author page: biography, the full Dune series in order, and "Influenced" (P2, P3a, P4, P5).
- The overall look: dark theme, serif titles, sage-green buttons and big covers (all six).
- The graph as an idea: "mind map," "skill tree" (P1, P2).
- Goodreads/StoryGraph import and export (P3a, P5).

## Recurring issues, by how many participants raised them

1. **"Recently looked at" shows raw ISBNs** instead of titles and covers (6/6).
2. **Subject chips are noisy:** raw tags like `Nyt:Mass-Market-Monthly=2021-11-07` and `Award:Nebula_Award=Novel`, near-duplicates (Science Fiction / Science-Fiction), and unexplained OL/WD/LC source labels (6/6).
3. **Subject-page recommendations are mostly old public-domain classics** (Alice in Wonderland, Dracula, Frankenstein, The Wizard of Oz) (6/6).
4. **Find Connection is unexplained.** It opens an empty "Connect Dune to…" box (6/6).
5. **Blank cover placeholders** in "More by Frank Herbert" in My Library (6/6).
6. **Search returns "Summary of…" knockoffs, then unrelated Shakespeare plays,** below the real book (5/6).
7. **My Library layout:** export and theme settings are buried below the recommendations (P1, P3b, P4).
8. **Thin data for non-fiction and comics:** Atomic Habits shows no author and three subjects. Saga leaves out Fiona Staples and series/volume info (P2, P3b, P5).
9. **The graph header is truncated** to "Explore Gra" behind the header buttons (P2, P3a).
10. **No book description** on the book page (P4).
11. **No reading status or star rating** for tracker-style use (P5).
12. **The library-URL setup explanation is hard to follow** (P2, P4).

## Light mode follow-up

The same six participants were shown the same session again, with the phone's color scheme set to light (the app's default "System" theme follows it). That gave 32 screenshots across home, search, Dune (saved), author, subject, graph, Find Connection, Saga, Atomic Habits, scan, My Library and the Edit panel. They answered styling questions only, and compared light mode against the dark session they had already seen.

### 1) Overall opinion of the light design

- **P1:** Like a page in a real book: cream paper, black serif titles. The dark green buttons look "way more serious and grown-up" than dark mode's mint ones, and covers pop with their soft shadow.
- **P2:** Cleaner than he expected. It reads like an old paperback page, and covers pop more. It's calmer and less "techy" than dark.
- **P3a:** Really likes it. It feels like a bookstore display, and the forest-green buttons look "more confident and grown-up."
- **P3b:** Warmer and more like paper, like a nice hardcover. Covers look "like books on a shelf instead of floating in the dark." It looks finished and calm.
- **P4:** Feels like reading a real book. The deep green buttons "say press me," and the Science Fiction list "looks like a nice library shelf."
- **P5:** Feels like an indie bookstore rather than a tech app. The author page with its stack of Dune covers is "probably the prettiest screen in either theme."

### 2) Easier or harder to see than in dark mode

**Easier:**
- **Primary buttons:** white text on dark green ("Scan a book", "View as Graph", "Find Connection", "Grant Permission") reads better than dark mode's dark text on mint (all six).
- **Graph nodes:** navy author, green book and rust subjects look crisper and richer on cream (all six).
- **Green text links:** "Frank Herbert", "Search on WorldCat" and "Edit subjects, genres & notes…" read clearly as tappable (P2, P4, P5).
- **Subject chips:** peach with rust text, and the blue topic chips on Science Fiction, are clearer than dark mode's "muddy brown blobs" (P1, P3a, P5).

**Harder:**
- **Grey secondary pills** ("My Library", "New Search", "Share", "Save", "Edit", "Filter", "List", "Export CSV") nearly melt into the cream and don't look like buttons (all six).
- **Empty cover placeholders** in search results and "More by Frank Herbert" almost vanish and "look like a page that didn't finish loading." In dark mode they read as deliberate placeholders (all six).
- **The "Not yet linked" legend dot** on the graph is barely visible (P1, P3a, P5).
- **Graph node labels** are still tiny in either theme (P2, P3a, P3b, P4, P5).
- **Small grey text** ("shares: author Frank Herbert", the Libraries paragraph) and the OL/WD/LC tags are faint (P4).

### 3) What they don't like about the light styling

- **The selected "System" chip under Appearance** is only a pale butter-yellow, so it's hard to tell which theme is picked (all six).
- **The pale pink "Remove" pill** doesn't read as a delete or warning action (P2, P3b, P5).
- **Pale-on-pale chips:** subject chips on Dune and Saga look faded rather than clickable, and the grey chips on the Edit screen blend "into one big beige block" (P2, P3b, P4).
- **"★ Saved" is rust text on a faint grey pill** and "doesn't feel like I did anything." In dark mode the orange glowed (P3a).
- **Mostly empty screens** (Scan, Find Connection, the ISBN-only recent list) look extra blank or unfinished against all the cream (P1, P5).
- **Header titles covered by the nav pills** ("Explore Gra…", "Find Conne…") are more noticeable on cream (P5).

### 4) Words that come to mind (light mode)

- **P1:** cozy, papery, calm, clean, old-fashioned, library card
- **P2:** cozy, paper, coffee-shop, library-ish, chill, kinda grown-up
- **P3a:** paper, calm, clean, bookish, cozy-café, a little faded
- **P3b:** warm, papery, calm, bookish, a little faded around the edges
- **P4:** warm, papery, gentle, calm, a little faded
- **P5:** cozy, paper, bookstore, calm, forest green, a little washed out

The most repeated words were *paper/papery* (6), *calm* (5), *cozy* (4) and *faded/washed out* (4). In dark mode the most repeated words were *nerdy* and *cluttered*.

### 5) Light or dark?

| Participant | Preference | When they'd use each |
|---|---|---|
| P1 (13) | Light (if she could only have one) | Light at the library; dark in bed at night |
| P2 (18) | Dark | Dark at night ("feels more sci-fi"); light in a bright comic shop or outdoors. Dark would "win easily" if its mint buttons were fixed |
| P3a (22) | Light for everyday | Light in the day and at bookstores; dark in bed |
| P3b (35) | Light for daytime | Light at lunch or in a bookstore; dark for 3 a.m. feeds |
| P4 (68) | Light | Light in the day, at the bookstore and at book club; dark in bed |
| P5 (29) | Light as daytime default | Light in the day, closer to Hardcover/StoryGraph; dark in bed. Light would be her "clear favorite" with stronger pills and placeholders |

Five of six prefer light for daytime. Everyone wants dark at night. The only participant who prefers dark overall is P2.

### Light mode styling fixes, by how many participants raised them

1. **Give the grey secondary pills more contrast** (a border or a darker fill) so they read as buttons (6/6).
2. **Make empty cover placeholders visible**, or show a book icon or the title initial, so they don't look like a failed load (6/6).
3. **Make the selected Appearance option obvious**, not a faint yellow (6/6).
4. **Darken the "Not yet linked" legend dot**, and enlarge graph node labels in both themes (3/6 and 5/6).
5. **Make "Remove" read as destructive** and "★ Saved" read as a clear confirmed state (3/6 and 1/6).
6. **Dark mode in return:** its mint primary buttons with dark text were weaker than light mode's (all six noticed when comparing).

## Verified bug

P5 suspected that Export CSV turns subjects into Goodreads shelves. This is confirmed in the code: [csv.ts:59](../apps/app/src/csv.ts#L59) writes every subject and genre into the `Bookshelves` column. Importing a single Dune export would create around 25 shelves in Goodreads, such as "Award:Hugo_Award=1966".
