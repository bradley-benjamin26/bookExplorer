# Copilot User Report — Light Theme

Companion to [copilotReview.md](./copilotReview.md), which covers the first round of feedback (dark theme, overall usability, and naming).

## Method

The same six personas were shown the app in its **light theme** (the "Standard" option under My Library → Appearance). They were given 26 phone-sized screenshots of the same screens and data as before, including the lower parts of each page. They also had the earlier dark-theme screenshots to compare against. Questions covered **visual styling only**: colour, contrast, readability, typography, spacing, chips, the graph, and covers. Naming questions were not asked.

The light theme in brief: a warm cream background and charcoal serif headings (Fraunces) with sans-serif body text (Inter). Primary buttons are deep green, secondary buttons are pale grey pills, "★ Saved" is rust, book-page subject chips are rust on peach, and subject-page chips are blue on lavender with small "WD"/"LC" source badges. Graph nodes are colour-coded by type, and a sticky header and footer frame every screen.

Questions:
1. First impression of the light theme
2. Light or dark: which would you use, and when?
3. Readability: anything too small, faint, or low-contrast?
4. Do the colours work together and help show what's tappable?
5. Best and worst-looking screen
6. What would you change?
7. Words that describe the light look

## Participant feedback

### Participant 1 — 13, frequent library visitor
1. **First impression:** Warm and bookish, "like a page in a nice reading journal". The big serif headings feel welcoming. She likes that it isn't bright white, but the huge empty space on the home screen feels odd.
2. **Light or dark:** Light during the day, because it's cheerful and makes covers pop. Dark at night, because it's gentler on the eyes.
3. **Readability:** The main text is clear. Hard to read: the graph hint text and node labels, the rating details on the *Dune* page, and the faint source badges on subject chips. Blank grey cover boxes in search and the library make rows harder to scan.
4. **Colours:** The green buttons are clearly tappable. The rust "Saved" label and subject chips feel warm and easy to spot. The blue chips are distinct, and the graph colours help tell node types apart.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the *Dune* book page, where the bright cover, warm chips, and green links go together. Worst is the graph, with small labels and crowded circles and lines.
6. **Changes:** Bigger, darker graph labels and easier-to-see grey helper text. Less blank missing-cover boxes and less extra empty space.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, calm, colorful, spacious.

### Participant 2 — 18, comics and sci-fi fan (usually uses dark mode)
1. **First impression:** Warm and bookish, like a page. A lot brighter than the dark look, and he'd notice the screen glow at night.
2. **Light or dark:** Dark most of the time, especially at night. Light in daylight, or when he wants covers and colours to pop.
3. **Readability:** Grey details like ratings and "shares: author…" feel faint. Graph labels are really small and crowded. Pale cover placeholders blend into the background.
4. **Colours:** Green buttons clearly read as the main actions. Rust "Saved" and the book-page chips belong together. The blue chips and graph colours separate categories, but the graph's small labels make the colours do too much of the work.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the *Dune* page, with the bright cover against the warm background. Worst is My Library, where empty grey cover boxes make the screen feel unfinished.
6. **Changes:** Darker small grey text and bigger, roomier graph labels. A clearer look for empty covers and a smaller sticky footer.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, clean, soft, bright.

### Participant 3a — 22, recent college graduate
1. **First impression:** Warm, calm, and bookish. The cream and serif headings are more inviting than stark white. The home screen has a lot of empty space.
2. **Light or dark:** Light during the day, browsing between things at work. Dark at night, which is easier on the eyes in low light.
3. **Readability:** Small grey details are faint, such as ratings and "shares: author Frank Herbert" on the book and library screens. Graph node labels are particularly small and crowded. Empty cover boxes in search blend in.
4. **Colours:** Green buttons stand out as actions. Blue subject-page chips are distinct from the rust book chips. The graph colours make sense with the legend. However, rust is used for both "Saved" and subjects, so it doesn't signal one clear meaning, and the pale chips don't look as obviously tappable as the buttons.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the *Dune* page, with its colourful cover, clear hierarchy, and polished green links. Worst is the graph, where tiny labels crowd around the nodes.
6. **Changes:** Darker muted text and larger, roomier graph labels. Less prominent missing-cover placeholders. Use the home screen's empty space to show the recent book **by title instead of a raw ISBN**.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, calm, readable, a little sparse.

### Participant 3b — 35, working new parent
1. **First impression:** Warm and bookish. The cream feels softer than white, and the dark serif headings are inviting. The green buttons stand out without being loud.
2. **Light or dark:** Light in daylight. Dark for late-night feeds in a dim room. She wants switching between them to be easy.
3. **Readability:** Headings and titles are comfortable to read. Graph labels are tiny and hard to scan. Muted grey helper text in My Library and the rating details on the book page look faint.
4. **Colours:** Green buttons are easy to spot. Blue chips are distinct from rust chips. Rust "Saved" and rust subject chips look related to each other. The graph's colours separate node types better than its small labels identify them.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the book page, with its large cover, clear title, and roomy spacing that's quick to take in. Worst is the graph, where small labels and many lines feel cramped.
6. **Changes:** Darker grey text and larger graph labels for quick one-handed glances. More breathing room in the graph and a smaller sticky footer.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, calm, spacious, colorful.

### Participant 4 — 68, retired and joining book clubs
1. **First impression:** Warm and bookish, "like cream paper with handsome dark headings". **Calmer and more welcoming than the dark theme.**
2. **Light or dark:** Light during the day and for longer reading, because it feels open and familiar. Maybe dark at night, though bright text on dark can feel stark.
3. **Readability:** Main text and large headings are easy to pick out. Graph node labels are small and crowded. Rating details on the book page and the faded source badges on chips take effort to read.
4. **Colours:** Deep green buttons clearly mean "tap". Grey secondary pills are distinct without competing. Rust "Saved" and the peach chips are pleasant but **a little soft against their backgrounds**. The blue chips and graph colours are easier to tell apart.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the book page, with a large cover, prominent title, and comfortable spacing. Worst is the graph, where small labels bunch around the nodes.
6. **Changes:** Larger graph labels with more room. Darker faint text and **darker rust chip lettering**. Fewer big empty gaps and less noticeable blank cover boxes.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, calm, gentle, spacious.

### Participant 5 — 29, heavy Goodreads user
1. **First impression:** Warm, calm, and bookish, and more inviting than stark white. It feels polished, but pages are a little too spacious, especially lists full of empty placeholder covers.
2. **Light or dark:** Light during the day, because it's easier to scan covers and lists quickly. Dark at night. Dark feels moodier.
3. **Readability:** Muted grey helper text on Home and Library is faint. The tiny source labels on Science Fiction chips are easy to miss. The graph is hardest to read, with small, crowded node labels and hint text that takes effort.
4. **Colours:** Green clearly marks primary actions. The rust "Saved" marker and peach chips are cohesive with the warm background. Blue chips are distinct. In the graph, the many rust subject nodes look alike, and the faded source badges are weak.
5. **Best / worst:** Best is the *Dune* page, where the vivid cover gives a focal point and green, rust, and cream work together. Worst is the graph, which is cramped and hard to read at a glance.
6. **Changes:** Darker muted grey text and chip source labels, and larger or roomier graph labels. Less prominent recommendation placeholders while covers load.
7. **Words:** Warm, bookish, calm, polished, spacious.

## What came up across participants

- **The light theme is well liked:** All six described it as **warm** and **bookish**, and five also said **calm**. Several said the cream background is softer and more inviting than stark white. Participant 4 found it more welcoming than the dark theme.
- **Light vs dark:** Five of six would use light during the day and dark at night, and several asked that switching stay easy. Participant 2 prefers dark overall, and Participant 4 leans toward light.
- **Best screen:** All six named the **book page**. The vivid cover against cream, with green and rust accents and roomy spacing, is the strongest screen.
- **Worst screen:** Five of six named the **graph**, because node labels are tiny and crowded and the hint text is hard to read. Participant 2 named My Library because of its blank cover boxes.
- **Colours:** Everyone said the **deep green buttons clearly signal "tap"**, and the blue subject-page chips are distinct from the rust book chips. Concerns:
  - Rust means both "Saved" and "subject", so it has no single meaning (3a, 3b).
  - Pale chips look less tappable than buttons (3a).
  - Rust and peach chip text is a little soft against its background (4).
  - Graph subject nodes all look alike (5).
- **Readability problems:**
  - Small grey helper text (rating details, "shares: author…") is too faint. Six of six said so.
  - Graph node labels are too small. Six of six said so.
  - Faded "WD"/"LC" source badges are hard to see (1, 4, 5).
- **Other visual issues:**
  - **Empty pale-grey placeholder covers** in My Library and search make screens look unfinished (all six).
  - Excess empty space, especially on the home screen (1, 3a, 4, 5).
  - The sticky footer takes up too much room (2, 3b).
  - "Recently looked at" shows a raw ISBN instead of the book's title (3a).
- **Suggested changes, by frequency:**
  1. Darken the muted grey text and chip source badges.
  2. Enlarge the graph labels and give them more space.
  3. Improve or hide placeholder covers.
  4. Tighten the empty space.
  5. Shrink the sticky footer.
  6. Show titles in "Recently looked at".
